The people behind the science

Notable Chemists

1743 – 1794
Antoine Lavoisier
Father of Modern Chemistry

Established conservation of mass, named oxygen and hydrogen. His Traité Élémentaire de Chimie was the first modern chemistry textbook.

1834 – 1907
Dmitri Mendeleev
Periodic Table Creator

Arranged elements by atomic weight and predicted properties of undiscovered elements. His gaps in the table were validated over decades.

1867 – 1934
Marie Curie
Radioactivity Pioneer

The only person to win Nobel Prizes in two sciences. Discovered polonium and radium; coined the term "radioactivity."

1901 – 1994
Linus Pauling
Chemical Bonding Theory

Introduced electronegativity, described chemical bonding quantum mechanically, and won two unshared Nobel Prizes.

Essential Chemistry

Key Reactions to Know

The reactions that shaped chemistry — from the laboratory to the industrial scale.

Combustion
CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

The complete combustion of methane — the primary component of natural gas — releasing 890 kJ/mol.

Neutralisation
HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

A strong acid reacts with a strong base to form a neutral salt and water. ΔH ≈ −57 kJ/mol.

Photosynthesis
6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

The endothermic conversion of carbon dioxide and water into glucose, driven by light energy in chlorophyll.

Haber Process
N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃

The industrial synthesis of ammonia at 450°C and 200 atm. Feeds approximately half the global population today.
