Watch ionic and covalent bonding happen in real time. Understand WHY each bond type forms, what drives electron transfer or sharing, and how it determines molecular properties.

Key Takeaways
  • Ionic bonds form by electron TRANSFER — metal gives electrons to non-metal (electronegativity difference > 1.7)
  • Covalent bonds form by electron SHARING — both atoms pull on the same pair of electrons
  • Bond polarity depends on electronegativity difference; molecule polarity depends on geometry too
  • Bond type determines melting point, conductivity, solubility, and reactivity
  • All bonding is a spectrum — no bond is 100% ionic or 100% covalent

Ionic Bonding

Example: Sodium Chloride (NaCl)
What happens
A metal atom loses one or more electrons to become a positive ion (cation). A non-metal atom gains those electrons to become a negative ion (anion). The opposite charges attract strongly.
Why it happens
The WHY
Na has 1 valence electron (weakly held — low ionisation energy). Cl needs 1 electron to complete its octet (high electron affinity). The overall energy change is highly negative — thermodynamically very favourable. ΔH_lattice for NaCl ≈ -787 kJ/mol.
Key formula
Na → Na⁺ + e⁻  (ionisation)
Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻  (electron gain)
Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl  (lattice forms)
Properties it causes
High melting point (strong lattice forces), conducts only when molten/dissolved (ions must be mobile), brittle (ion layers slide — same charges align and repel), soluble in polar solvents like water.
⚠ Common Mistake
NaCl does NOT contain molecules — it has no discrete NaCl units. It's a giant ionic lattice where each Na⁺ is surrounded by 6 Cl⁻ and vice versa. The formula unit NaCl shows the simplest ratio, not a molecule.
Real-world relevance
NaCl regulates nerve impulse transmission. Na⁺ influx depolarises the neuron membrane — every thought, heartbeat, and muscle movement depends on ionic gradients.
Ionic Bond — Energy Profile (Born-Haber Concept)

The lattice energy (−787 kJ/mol for NaCl) is the dominant energy release — it makes ionic bond formation highly thermodynamically favourable overall.
