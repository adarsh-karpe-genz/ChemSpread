What is Chemistry?

Chemistry is the science of matter — its composition, structure, properties, and the changes it undergoes. It operates at every scale, from single atoms to industrial processes producing millions of tonnes of product.

Chemistry is the central science: it connects physics (on one side) to biology and medicine (on the other). Understanding chemistry means understanding the material world.

Physical vs Chemical Changes

Physical Change

No new substance formed. Chemical composition unchanged. Usually reversible.

  • → Ice melting → water (H₂O → H₂O)
  • → Sugar dissolving in water
  • → Cutting a piece of copper wire
  • → Crushing a tin can

Chemical Change

New substance with different properties forms. Usually irreversible. Energy absorbed or released.

  • → Iron rusting: 4Fe + 3O₂ → 2Fe₂O₃
  • → Burning wood: C + O₂ → CO₂
  • → Cooking an egg (protein denaturation)
  • → HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

Elements, Compounds & Mixtures

Pure Substance

Element

Made of only one type of atom. Cannot be broken down chemically. 118 known elements.

Fe, Au, O₂, N₂, C, Na, Cl₂
Pure Substance

Compound

Two or more elements chemically bonded in fixed ratios. Different properties from constituent elements.

H₂O, NaCl, CO₂, C₆H₁₂O₆
Impure Substance

Mixture

Two or more substances physically combined. No fixed ratio. Separable physically.

Air, sea water, crude oil, alloys

Conservation Laws

Conservation of Mass

Total mass of reactants = total mass of products in any chemical reaction. (Lavoisier, 1789)

m(reactants) = m(products)

Conservation of Energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only converted. Exothermic: releases energy. Endothermic: absorbs energy.

ΔU_system + ΔU_surroundings = 0
